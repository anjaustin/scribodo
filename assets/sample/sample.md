# The Lighthouse at Ørnes

Chapter One — draft

The fog came in off the fjord the way it always did in late October: quietly, completely, and without asking. Zoë Ångström stood at the top of the lighthouse stairs with a lantern in one hand and her grandmother’s letter in the other, and she thought, not for the first time, that she had made a mistake in coming back.

“You’re late,” said Dvořák, without turning around. He was hunched over the old Fresnel lens, polishing a brass fitting that did not need polishing. “The ferry was on time. I watched it.”

“The ferry was on time,” she agreed. “I wasn’t.”

He grunted — the particular grunt that meant *we will talk about this later, and you will not enjoy it* — and went back to his polishing.

## A long paragraph for wrapping

She remembered the summer she was nine, when the keeper’s cottage still had its red door and her grandmother still kept bees in the walled garden behind it; she remembered the smell of tar and woodsmoke, and the way the beam swept across the ceiling of the little attic room every eleven seconds, so regular that she had learned to count herself to sleep by it, one-two-three-four-five-six-seven-eight-nine-ten-eleven, light, one-two-three, and how, on the night the beam stopped, she had lain awake in the sudden total darkness and understood, with the strange clarity children sometimes have, that something had changed that would not change back.
